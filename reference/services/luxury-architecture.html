Luxury home architecture for bespoke new homes and complete transformations

You have a plot, a house you want to replace, or a home you want to change fundamentally, and you want it designed properly. That is the situation most clients on this page arrive in, and it is the work this practice was set up to do.

Bespoke homes

Hawkstone is an architectural design and planning practice for homes. Luxury residential architects across Derbyshire, Staffordshire and Cheshire, from first sketch through planning, technical design and construction.

New build house architects: bespoke homes on your own plot

We design bespoke new build homes for private clients and self-build plots, including custom build sites with a design code. Planning comes first: what the site allows, what the local authority expects, and whether the plot sits in the Green Belt or open countryside. On qualifying rural sites, Paragraph 84 allows an isolated home of exceptional design quality, and we have worked that route. Contemporary, traditional or a blend of both: the site usually decides.

Replacement dwellings and knock-down rebuilds

Sometimes the best plot is under a house you already own. Where the existing property is poorly built or the wrong house for its site, replacing it can make more sense than repairing it. Replacement dwelling policy is tightest in the Green Belt, where the new house is generally expected to be no larger than the old one. There is more room in that than the wording suggests, but only with a design-led case, and we establish what is achievable before you commit.

Complete redesigns and substantial extensions of existing homes

Not every transformation starts with demolition. Many clients own a house with good bones and the wrong plan: a period farmhouse, a mid-century bungalow, a family home that has stopped working. We redesign the whole house, not one room: reorganising the plan, opening it to garden and light, adding significant space and bringing fabric and services up to new-build standard.

Is this the right service
for your project?

It’s for you if:

—

You own or are buying a plot in Derbyshire, Staffordshire or Cheshire and want an architect-designed home on it.

—

You own a house you want to replace, or to transform so completely that it becomes a different home.

—

You want one practice to design it, gain planning and see it through.

You want a house designed for your site and the way you live, not a plan adapted from somewhere else.

It probably isn’t if:

You’re looking for a luxury home to buy. We design homes and gain planning for them; we don’t build or sell property.

—

You need a single-room extension or a loft conversion. We can point you to the right people.

—

Your house is listed or in a conservation area. We do that work, but it starts as a heritage project. Listed Buildings

—

You have a barn or agricultural building to convert. Barn Conversions

Feasibility before design

The most expensive mistake in this type of project is designing a scheme the building can’t support or the council won’t approve. So we start with feasibility:

1

Feasibility consultation.

A fixed-fee, 60-minute session with a lead designer, with a desktop review of your site beforehand and a written report afterwards.

2

Brief and surveys.

We agree the aspirations, priorities, budget and architectural character, arrange the topographical and building surveys, and bring in any specialists the project needs: planning, landscape, interiors, ecology.

3

Concept design.

Layout, scale, appearance and the overall direction of the house, developed through drawings and 3D modelling so the design can be judged as spaces rather than plans.

4

Planning & design development.

Materials and key details refined with you, and the planning application prepared, coordinated and managed through to decision.

5

Technical design.

Construction drawings, specifications and coordination with structural engineers, energy consultants and other specialists, so the house can be priced and built accurately.

6

Construction support.

Contractor selection, design queries answered on site, and oversight through the build where you appoint us for it.

FAQs

Questions we’re asked about bespoke and luxury homes

  • How much does it cost to design a luxury home?

    Architectural fees are usually a percentage of construction cost or a fixed fee agreed per stage, and they scale with the complexity of the house rather than its label. The construction cost itself is driven by size, specification and site conditions. We give budget pointers at the feasibility stage and design to an agreed figure from the brief onwards.

  • What is included in a luxury residential architectural design service?

    Feasibility advice, measured surveys, concept and developed design, 3D visualisation, planning submissions, technical drawings and specifications, and coordination of the consultants the project needs. Construction-stage support is available where you want it. Each appointment is tailored to the project.

    ‍

  • Do I need planning permission to build a new home?

    Yes. A new house always requires full planning permission, and a replacement dwelling does too. In the Green Belt and open countryside the policy tests are stricter, and the design has to make a specific case. We establish the likely route and its risks before you commit to design work.

  • How long does it take to design and build a bespoke home?

    Realistically, design and planning take a matter of months, with a full planning application typically determined in eight to thirteen weeks once submitted. Technical design follows approval, and construction depends on the size of the house. We set out a programme at the feasibility stage.

  • Can you design a bespoke home around our budget?

    Yes, and we would rather know the budget at the start. We establish it at the brief stage, design to it, and build cost reviews into the process so the figure is tested as the design develops rather than at tender.

  • Do you design both contemporary and traditional homes?

    Yes. Our projects range from contemporary houses to traditional ones and to considered blends of the two. We don’t impose a house style; the site, its context and your brief decide it.

  • Can you redesign or extend our existing house instead of replacing it?

    Yes. Where a house has good bones, a complete redesign with substantial new space can be the better route, and it is a large part of our work. Where the existing house is the problem, replacement can be more sensible. A feasibility consultation is where we look at both.

  • Do you manage the project through construction?

    Where you appoint us for it. We can assist with contractor selection, respond to design queries and oversee the work on site so that what gets built is what was designed. You can also appoint us for the design and planning stages alone.
